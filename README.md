# Corporate Commute Analytics (SQL)

SQL analysis of 144,578 employee cab trips for a fictional corporate commute company, **CommuteCo**, to find what drives no-shows, low ratings and no-show disputes, and what to fix first.

> **Note:** all data is synthetic, generated by `generate_data.py`. The project is inspired by real problems in enterprise mobility SaaS; no company or customer data is used.

## The business problem

CommuteCo's operations team sees rising complaints about employees being marked "no-show" unfairly, slow dispute resolution and uneven vendor quality. They want to know:

1. How healthy is the service overall, and is it improving?
2. Which shifts, sites and vendors drive no-shows?
3. How much does cab punctuality affect rider experience?
4. How big is the no-show dispute problem, and where does it come from?
5. Which employees are at risk of being blocked from booking?

## Dataset

| Table | Rows | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `trips` | 144,578 | One row per trip: employee, site, vendor, date, shift, login/logout, cab delay, status |
| `feedback` | 40,624 | Rider rating (1-5) and issue category for rated trips |
| `disputes` | 1,472 | No-show disputes raised by employees, with resolution time and outcome |
| `employees` | 2,400 | Site, department and shift |
| `sites`, `vendors` | 3, 4 | Lookup tables |

Period: July to September 2026. Schema: [`sql/01_schema.sql`](sql/01_schema.sql).

## Key findings

**1. One vendor causes most wrong no-show marks.** CityWheels runs 33% of trips but is behind 57% of disputes where the employee was proven right. Its cabs arrive 11.7 minutes late on average, only 22% arrive within 5 minutes, and its rating is 3.68 against 4.3-4.4 for the others.

![Vendor share of trips vs wrong no-show marks](images/vendor_wrong_marks.png)

**2. Late cabs create no-shows.** When the cab is more than 10 minutes late, the no-show rate doubles (4.5% vs 2.2%). Many of these are employees who gave up waiting or were marked absent at the wrong time.

**3. Ratings collapse after 10 minutes of delay.** Ratings hold at about 4.4 up to 5 minutes late, then drop to 3.6 at 11-20 minutes and 2.8 beyond 20 minutes, where 36% of riders give 1 or 2 stars.

![Average rating by cab delay](images/delay_vs_rating.png)

**4. Night shifts are the riskiest.** The 22:00 and 02:00 shifts have no-show rates of about 3.7%, roughly 60% higher than day shifts (about 2.3%).

![No-show rate by shift](images/no_show_by_shift.png)

**5. Site B has a pickup-point problem.** At Site B, 45% of no-shows are disputed and 74% of disputes are accepted, far above Sites A and C (about 31% disputed, 50-54% accepted). This points to a location issue (wrong gate, hard-to-find pickup point), not employee behaviour.

**6. Disputes are slow.** The median dispute takes 80 hours to resolve and 88% take longer than 24 hours.

**7. 352 employees hit 3 no-shows within 30 days**, the typical threshold for a booking block. Given findings 1 and 5, a large share of them may be blocked unfairly.

**8. Mondays have double the cancellations** (6.1% vs about 3% on other weekdays), suggesting rosters are not updated after the weekend.

## Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Expected impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Put CityWheels on a punctuality SLA** (e.g., 80% within 5 minutes) and shift volume to SwiftRide and GreenFleet if it is missed | Fewer late cabs, fewer wrong no-shows, higher ratings |
| 2 | **Verify cab arrival before a no-show can be marked** (geofence + wait time + auto-call), and add a one-tap dispute with evidence | Prevents wrong marks at the source; disputes resolved in hours, not days |
| 3 | **Audit Site B pickup points** (gates, landmarks, geofence radius) | Cuts the site with the highest wrong-mark rate |
| 4 | **Send a roster-confirmation reminder on Sunday evening** | Reduces Monday cancellations and wasted trips |
| 5 | **Review the 352 at-risk employees' no-shows before any block** applies | Avoids penalising employees for vendor or location errors |

Recommendation 2 is developed in detail in my PRD: [Reducing No-Show Disputes](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JS70gjF0Kpv0nGKaOV3FfcWrGBCXJ50pkdcI-rfsYiI/edit?usp=sharing).

## SQL techniques used

Aggregation with conditional counts, CASE bucketing, multi-table joins, CTEs, window functions (`LAG`, `ROW_NUMBER`, `SUM() OVER()`), rolling-window logic (3 no-shows in 30 days), median calculation, week-over-week trends and cohort retention. All queries are in [`sql/02_analysis.sql`](sql/02_analysis.sql), each answering one business question.

## How to run

```bash
pip install matplotlib
python generate_data.py   # creates data/*.csv
python load_sqlite.py     # loads them into commute.db (SQLite)
python run_queries.py     # runs every query and prints results
python make_charts.py     # regenerates the charts in images/
```

The queries use SQLite syntax; comments note the PostgreSQL equivalents where they differ.
